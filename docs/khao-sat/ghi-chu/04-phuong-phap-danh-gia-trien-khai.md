## Q1. Exemplar industrial experience papers: what they measured and their key numbers

### Takeaway
The canonical papers (Google Tricorder/CACM 2018, Facebook Infer/Zoncolan CACM 2019) do not judge a tool by precision against ground truth. They judge it by **developer action in the workflow**: "effective false positives", fix/action rate, and Please-fix vs Not-useful clicks. Their strongest single lesson is that the same analyzer gets about 0% fixes in batch mode and over 70% at diff/PR time. Recent LLM code-review papers from 2024–2026 (Beko/Qodo, Atlassian, ByteDance, Ericsson, Google, Tencent) keep this "resolution rate" tradition and add precision from manual labeling, cost per alarm, and PR cycle time.

### Cited Findings

**Google, CACM 2018. Sadowski, Aftandilian, Eagle, Miller-Cushon, Jaspan, "Lessons from Building Static Analysis Tools at Google", CACM 61(4):58–66 [V]**
- Definition (exact): an issue is an "effective false positive" if "developers did not take positive action after seeing the issue." If an analysis wrongly reports an issue but developers fix it anyway, that is *not* an effective false positive. If it reports a real fault that the developer did not understand, it *is* one. "Developers, not tool authors, will determine and act on a tool's perceived" value. — [CACM PDF (MIT mirror)](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/lessons-from-building-static-analysis-tools-at-google/)
- Code-review checks "are allowed to include up to 10% effective false positives". Criteria for a check are: understandable; actionable and easy to fix; "Produce less than 10% effective false positives. Developers should feel the check is pointing out an actual issue at least 90% of the time"; and potential for significant impact. Compile-time checks must produce **no** effective false positives. — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf)
- Feedback instrumentation: reviewers can click "Please fix", and anyone can click "Not useful", which files a bug to the analyzer owner. "The Tricorder team tracks such not-useful clicks, computing the ratio of 'Please fix' vs. 'Not useful' clicks. If the ratio for an analyzer goes above 10%, the Tricorder team disables the analyzer until the author(s) improve it." — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf)
- Scale as of January 2018: about 50,000 code-review changes analyzed per day, with up to 3 analysis runs per second at peak. Reviewers clicked "Please Fix" more than 5,000 times/day, authors applied automated fixes about 3,000 times/day, and analyzers received "Not useful" clicks 250 times/day. — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf)
- Batch-triage failure: in the May 2009 FindBugs "Fixit", engineers reviewed 3,954 warnings (42% of 9,473). Only 16% (640) were fixed, even though 44% (1,746) led to a bug report. FindBugs code-review integration was dropped in 2011 because "effective false positives caused developers to lose confidence in the tool" and per-developer customization caused inconsistent views. — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf)
- Lessons listed: finding bugs is easy; most developers will not go out of their way to use static analysis; developer happiness is key; "do not just find bugs, fix them"; crowdsource analysis development. — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf); [acawiki summary](https://acawiki.org/Lessons_from_Building_Static_Analysis_Tools_at_Google)
- The predecessor paper, Sadowski et al., "Tricorder: Building a Program Analysis Ecosystem", ICSE 2015, is the source of the "effective false positive" concept that the CACM paper cites (ref. 35). **[K: I did not fetch it this session.]**

**Facebook/Meta, CACM 2019. Distefano, Fähndrich, Logozzo, O'Hearn, "Scaling Static Analyses at Facebook", CACM 62(8):62–70, DOI 10.1145/3338112 [V]**
- Exact: "the diff time deployment saw a 70% fix rate, where a more traditional 'offline' or 'batch' deployment (where bug lists are presented to engineers, outside their workflow) saw a 0% fix rate." — [CACM PDF (MIT mirror)](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf); [UCL open-access copy](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10084236/1/O%27Hearn%20AAM%20scaling-static-analysis-at-facebook.pdf)
- "Human factors" section: in the first (nightly batch) Infer deployment the team "assigned 20–30 issues to developers, and almost none of them were acted on." They had worked "to get the false positive rate down to what we thought was less than 20%, and yet the fix rate—the proportion of reported issues that developers resolved—was near zero." At diff time "the fix rate rocketed to over 70%. The same program analysis, with same false positive rate, had much greater impact." — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf)
- Metrics vocabulary: **actioned reports** ("At diff time an action is an update to the diff that removes a static analysis report") and **observable missed bugs** (bugs seen through bug bounty, security review, SEV review or crash logs that the analysis did not report). — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf)
- Zoncolan (security taint analysis): "43.3% of the severe security bugs are detected via Zoncolan". Its "action rate" is above 80%, with about 11 "missed bugs". 211 SEV-level issues were prevented at diff time versus 122 detected on master. — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf)
- The RacerD concurrency analysis had a fix rate of "roughly 50%", lower than the general diff analysis but valued because of bug severity. The authors use this to illustrate "balancing action rates and bug severity". — [CACM PDF](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf)

### Inferences
- For company Z, the headline metric should be the **action/resolution rate of findings surfaced at PR time**, defined the way Facebook, RovoDev and BitsAI-CR define it (the flagged line was changed or the alert closed by a code change within the window). Report it next to a **developer-judged effective-FP rate** using Google's definition and its <10% target as the benchmark.
- The Facebook 0% batch vs 70% diff-time contrast is directly testable in miniature. Give one batch report (for example a SARIF/HTML dump of existing debt on main) to developers and also run the tool on new PRs, then compare action rates. That gives a natural "deployment-model" comparison inside 2–3 weeks.
- For LLM-assisted triage (if the group adds an LLM filter on top of Semgrep/CodeQL), the Tencent template applies. Have company developers label a few hundred alarms as TP/FP, then report the FP reduction, the recall kept, $/alarm and s/alarm against an estimate of 10–20 min per alarm of manual triage.

## Q2. Metrics and study designs used, and the methodology guidelines they follow

### Takeaway
The metrics fall into four groups: (a) **action/outcome** (fix rate, action rate, resolution rate, outdated rate, time-to-fix); (b) **signal quality** (effective FP rate from developer feedback, precision from manual labeling, recall against known bugs or replays); (c) **cost/overhead** (CI time, $ and seconds per alarm, triage minutes, PR cycle time); and (d) **perception** (Likert surveys, interviews). Designs are typically observational case studies with a before/after or deployment-mode comparison. Reporting follows Runeson & Höst-style case-study guidelines and the ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards.

### Cited Findings
- **Fix rate** = "the proportion of reported issues that developers resolved" (Facebook). **Action rate** for diff time = an update to the diff that removes the report. — [Facebook CACM](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf)
- **Effective FP** = no positive action after seeing the issue. The threshold is under 10% for code review and 0% for compile-time checks. The operational proxy is the Not-useful/Please-fix click ratio, with an analyzer disabled if it exceeds 10%. — [Google CACM](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf)
- **Actionable alert ratio** (27–50%) and **alert lifespan / time-to-fix** (median 96 days) are measured by mining commits, and **fix complexity** by changed LOC. — [Imtiaz et al. ISSRE 2019](https://lists.ovirt.org/archives/list/devel@ovirt.org/message/EWA4T3NXV7JRXBX6SKRQNZ4WS2XZ76VU/attachment/4/issre19_cameraready.pdf)
- **Resolution rate of AI comments** (73.8%) is combined with per-PR surveys and a practitioner survey (n=22), and **PR closure time** is used as a side-effect metric. — [Cihan et al. ICSE-SEIP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18531)
- **Code resolution rate vs a human-comment baseline** (38.70% vs 44.45% [S]), **PR cycle time** (−30.8%), and **reduction in human comments** (−35.6%), using offline, online and user-feedback evaluation. — [RovoDev ICSE-SEIP 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01129)
- **Precision** from manual labeling (75.0%), **Outdated Rate** as the action proxy (26.7%), weekly active users, and a weekly precision time series. — [BitsAI-CR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15134)
- **FP elimination %, recall retention, $/alarm, s/alarm vs manual minutes/alarm**. — [Tencent arXiv 2601.18844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18844)
- **Detection rate on historical vulnerability-contributing commits** (52% single tool, 78% combined) and the **share of warnings unrelated to the vulnerability** (≥76%), measured by retrospective replay. — [Charoenwet et al. ISSTA 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12241)
- **SCA PR merge rate** (>70%) and **merge lag** (median about 4 h), triangulated with a survey (n=131). — [He et al. TSE 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07230)
- **CI build-break causes and time-to-resolve breaks**. — [Zampetti et al. MSR 2017](https://bibtex.github.io/MSR-2017-ZampettiSOCP.html)
- **ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards, Case Study standard: essential attributes** (exact): "justifies the selection of the case(s) or site(s) that was(were) studied"; "describes the site(s) in rich detail"; "the site is a real (physical or virtual) place, not a toy example or imaginary world"; "reports the type of case study"; "describes data sources (e.g. participants' demographics and work roles)"; "defines unit(s) of analysis or observation"; "presents a clear chain of evidence from observations to findings". — [CaseStudy.md on GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards/master/docs/standards/CaseStudy.md)
- **Desirable attributes** include: supplemental materials (interview guides, coding schemes); triangulation across data sources, informants or researchers; cross-checking interviewee statements against observation or archival records; member checking; describing external events that may have affected the case; reflecting on researcher bias; using quotations to illustrate findings. **Extraordinary attributes**: multiple deep cases; team-based analysis with inter-rater reliability; a pre-published protocol. — [CaseStudy.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards/master/docs/standards/CaseStudy.md)
- **Case Study anti-patterns** include "Relying on a single approach to data collection (e.g. interviews) without corroboration or data triangulation" and oversimplifying messy phenomena. The fetch summary also lists "Using a large language model to analyze data" as an anti-pattern. [V via fetch summary; re-check the exact wording, since it matters if the group uses LLMs to code survey answers.] — [CaseStudy.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards/master/docs/standards/CaseStudy.md)
- The standards are organized into essential, desirable and extraordinary attributes and cover about 19 method standards plus a General Standard. The General Standard covers research questions and validity (conclusion, construct, internal, reliability). — [SIGSOFT Empirical Standards site](https://www2.sigsoft.org/EmpiricalStandards/); [arXiv 2010.03525](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03525v1)
- [K, not re-fetched] Runeson, P. & Höst, M., "Guidelines for conducting and reporting case study research in software engineering", Empirical Software Engineering 14(2):131–164, 2009. This is the standard reference for case-study design: objective, case and units of analysis, data collection (direct, independent and archival), triangulation, and validity threats (construct, internal, external, reliability). Wohlin et al., *Experimentation in Software Engineering* (Springer, 2012) is the standard reference for validity-threat taxonomies.

### Inferences
- **Proposed metric set for a 2–3 week deployment at company Z** (each item can be computed and shared as an aggregate):
  1. *Coverage/volume*: repos and PRs scanned; findings per PR and per KLOC, split by tool type (SAST/SCA/secrets) and severity.
  2. *Action rate* (Facebook/RovoDev style): the share of PR-time findings whose flagged line was changed or the alert closed by a commit before merge. Report it separately from the batch backlog action rate to replicate the batch vs diff-time contrast.
  3. *Effective FP rate* (Google style): developer label per finding (Fix / Won't-fix-valid / False positive / Not understood). Under 10% is the external benchmark. Use a bot comment with reaction buttons or a label taxonomy.
  4. *Manual precision on a sample*: two raters (students plus one company developer) label a random sample of about 100–400 findings, and the report includes Cohen's kappa. Cohen's kappa is a "team-based approach / inter-rater reliability" item, an extraordinary attribute in the Case Study standard.
  5. *Retrospective recall proxy*: replay the tools on the last N merged PRs (for example 50–200) and count real issues, or known past incidents, that would have been caught. This follows Charoenwet's VCC replay.
  6. *Overhead*: median and p90 CI pipeline time delta, plus triage minutes per finding (self-reported). If an LLM is used, also $ per PR and $ per alarm (Tencent style).
  7. *Time-to-remediate* for secrets and critical SCA (hours), compared with merge lag norms (He et al.: median about 4 h for Dependabot merges).
  8. *Perception*: a short Likert survey after week 2 (TAM-style usefulness and ease-of-use items, optional SUS), plus 3–5 short interviews, to triangulate against the logs.
- **Design**: a single-case, embedded case study (units of analysis = PRs or findings) with a before/after or deployment-mode comparison. Three weeks is too short for interrupted time series, so present it as an *observational pre/post plus retrospective replay* and state the validity threats explicitly: novelty or Hawthorne effect, short duration, selection of repos.

## Q3. What counts as a credible small-scale industrial evaluation, and how papers handle confidentiality and company confirmation

### Takeaway
Credibility comes from **real workflow data, a clearly defined action metric, triangulation (logs + labels + survey), and honest limitations**, not from scale. Published industry-track papers rest on as few as 433 labeled alarms (Tencent), 3 projects / 1,568 PRs / 22 practitioners (Beko), "preliminary experiments with experienced developers" (Ericsson), or an anecdote of 20–30 assigned issues (Facebook's batch failure). Confidentiality is handled by publishing aggregate rates and anonymized excerpts, and the Case Study standard says that not disclosing confidential data is not a valid criticism.

### Cited Findings
- Tencent: 433 alarms (328 FP / 105 TP) across 3 bug types was enough for an industrial empirical study. Product codebases are named only at product level (Advertising and Marketing Services), with no code released. — [arXiv 2601.18844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18844)
- Beko/Qodo: 3 projects, 4,335 PRs (1,568 auto-reviewed), plus a survey of 22 practitioners, accepted at ICSE 2025 SEIP. — [arXiv 2412.18531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18531)
- Ericsson: an ICSME 2025 industry-track experience report based on "preliminary experiments with experienced developers". [S] — [arXiv 2507.19115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19115v2)
- Facebook's key human-factors evidence is the batch deployment where "20–30 issues" were assigned and "almost none" were acted on. Small numbers are acceptable when the contrast is stark and honestly reported. — [Facebook CACM](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/facebook-infer-cacm.pdf)
- The Case Study standard says that not disclosing data is not a valid criticism, because qualitative data are often confidential. It still expects participant demographics and roles to be described. It also says "The point of a case study is to study one thing deeply, not to generalize to a population" and that there is "no magic number" of interviews. — [CaseStudy.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards/master/docs/standards/CaseStudy.md)
- Industrial papers report percentages and rates (resolution %, cycle-time %, FP-elimination %) rather than raw code or repository names. Examples are RovoDev's −30.8% cycle time and −35.6% human comments, and BitsAI-CR's 75% precision. — [RovoDev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01129); [BitsAI-CR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15134)
- The Case Study standard's desirable attributes include "member checking", i.e. validating results with participants. That is the methodological hook for a **company confirmation step**. — [CaseStudy.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards/master/docs/standards/CaseStudy.md)

### Inferences
- **Credible minimum for company Z** (based on the exemplars above): at least 1–3 active repositories; all PRs over 2–3 weeks (likely tens to low hundreds); at least 100 findings labeled by company developers, plus a double-rated subset for kappa; a retrospective replay on historical merged PRs as a baseline; 5 or more survey responses and 3–5 interviews. Report the denominators everywhere.
- **Baselines** that fit in 3 weeks: (i) a replay on the previous N merged PRs or the previous month (tool off) compared with live PR-time (tool on); (ii) batch backlog report vs PR-time comments (the Facebook contrast); (iii) the company's existing linter or none; (iv) for LLM triage, raw SAST output vs LLM-filtered output on the same labeled set.
- **Confidentiality protocol**: compute metrics inside the company and export only an aggregated CSV (counts, rates, CI-time medians); anonymize the company as "Z" with only sector, size band, stack and team size; paraphrase or synthesize code examples; have the company review the paper before submission. **Company confirmation**: a signed letter or email from the CTO or engineering lead confirming the deployment dates, the scope, and that the reported aggregate numbers are accurate. Mention it in the paper ("results were reviewed and confirmed by Z's engineering management") and follow it with member checking of the findings. This protocol is my recommendation, not something I found codified in a single paper.

### Gaps
- I found no published paper that describes in detail a formal "company confirmation letter" procedure. The practice is inferred from member checking and from industry-track norms such as author affiliation and company approval for publication.
- arXiv has no specific confidentiality policy beyond general licensing; I did not research this.
